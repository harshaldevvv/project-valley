# Lore Bible v0: the Harrow Valley

Owner: narrative-designer. Names here are working names; change them here first, then in `src/content/text/`.

## Setting

The Harrow River runs west out of the Cascades through forty kilometres of fir, hop farms and logging towns before it reaches a tidal port. It rains nine months a year. Five years ago about 60,000 people lived in the valley. Now it holds a few thousand, depending on how you count.

The player arrives five years after the outbreak. The dead are old news. The interesting question is who is still alive, what they want, and why the far end of the valley glows on a clear night.

Landmarks: Harrow Dam, the KHRV radio mast, the Aurora observatory dome, the crashed C-130 on Teller Ridge, the port smokestacks.

## What happened

1. **Year 0, March.** Meridian Biosciences runs a contract programme at its Annex in the upper valley: cell regeneration for battlefield trauma. The Annex has its own small research reactor for power and isotope work. Locals know it as "the place with good parking and no signs".
2. **Week 1.** Three Annex staff are admitted to Fennick General with "flu that won't break". Two die. Both get up in the morgue. The night orderly's statement is the first written record and is filed as a psychiatric incident.
3. **Weeks 2–3.** Cases appear in Lowell Bend, then the port. The county calls it a meningitis cluster. Bend Crossing Mall becomes a shelter. On day eleven it stops answering the phone.
4. **Week 4.** The National Guard sets up Camp Teller and seals the valley road at the Ostrander bridge. The quarantine fence goes up around the upper valley. Evacuation buses run for six days, then stop without explanation.
5. **Month 2.** Teller loses its perimeter on a single night. After-action reports call it "a migration". The Guard pulls back to the airfield.
6. **Month 3, the pull-out.** A federal order reclassifies the valley "unrecoverable" and pulls the military out by air. The official reason is to preserve force. The unofficial reason, from radio logs, is that the Annex reactor was overheating with nobody left to run it, and nobody wanted to be downwind.
7. **Month 3, the burn.** The Annex reactor's coolant fails. A pool fire and a hydrogen blast breach the containment hall. Fallout settles over Zone K and the upper river. The army never comes back to clean it up.
8. **Years 1–5.** Survivors find each other. Trade routes form along the river. The dead keep coming down from the headwaters, and some that come down are changed: slower, tougher, warm to the touch.

What is deliberately unknown: whether the Annex made the infection or was only studying it. Notes should support both readings.

## Factions

### The Ledger (traders' guild)
- **Who:** truckers, a port logistics manager, two pawnbrokers and a former county auditor. Based in the grain terminal at Port Ostrander, with stalls at every safe zone.
- **Goal:** keep goods moving. A road that is open is worth more than a town that is held.
- **Attitude:** polite, neutral, keeps exact books. They will sell to anyone and remember everything you owe.
- **Trade flavour:** best prices on ammo and fuel. They buy serial-numbered items at a premium. Every receipt is stamped with a page number.
- **Voice:** "Nothing personal. It is all written down."

### The Wardens (militia)
- **Who:** ex-Guard who stayed behind, deputies and hunters. They hold Camp Teller's south gate and run checkpoints on the valley road.
- **Goal:** order. One law, one road, one armed authority. They want the valley re-fenced and the Annex sealed for good.
- **Attitude:** protective and controlling. They help newcomers and they also search newcomers. Siding with them cools the Ledger.
- **Trade flavour:** military gear, no haggling, paid partly in work. Their prices fall as your standing rises.
- **Voice:** "Hands where I can see them. Welcome to the valley."

### The Annex (lab survivors)
- **Who:** about twenty Meridian staff and the families who sheltered with them, living in Bunker 3 under the hill. Some are scientists, most were cooks, guards and IT.
- **Goal:** finish the work. A treatment, or at least an answer. They need samples, power and data drives from inside Zone K.
- **Attitude:** courteous, exhausted, guilty, and evasive about Year 0. The Wardens blame them for everything, and that is partly fair.
- **Trade flavour:** medical items, respirators and filters, keycards. They pay in knowledge: map reveals, radio frequencies and keycard codes.
- **Voice:** "We would rather not talk about the reactor. Do you have the drive?"

## Tone rules

- **Grounded first.** People ration, argue, keep lists and name their dogs. Writing about logistics beats writing about heroics.
- **Dry, a little dark.** The humour comes from people coping. It never comes from the gore. A note can be funny; a corpse is not the joke.
- **Specific beats general.** Write "Ana, the blue door, after dark", not "my loved one". Every note has a name, a place or a time.
- **No clichés as punchlines.** No "they're coming", no "I've been bitten" as the final line, no "it was us all along" reveals.
- **Never explain the zombies.** Characters guess. The guesses disagree.
- **Length:** notes under 120 words, radio under 45 seconds read aloud, item descriptions 1–2 lines.
- **Region voice:** T1 is personal and domestic, T3 transactional, T4 bureaucratic and military, and T5 clinical, with cracks.

## POI story briefs (examples)

### 1. Fennick Pharmacy (T1)
A pharmacist held out alone, rationing insulin to the town.
- Barricaded counter made of shelving units, with a sleeping bag and a camp stove behind it.
- A clipboard taped to the counter: names, doses and dates. The last dates are crossed out by a different hand.
- An empty insulin fridge with its door propped open, the power cord cut.
- A note to "Ana" pinned under a jar of lollipops: "Blue door, after dark. I kept yours."

### 2. Cold Spring Hop Farm (T1)
A family tried to keep farming through the first winter.
- Hop trellises half-harvested, and a tractor stalled mid-row with the door open.
- A kitchen table set for five, with three plates used and two clean.
- A child's chalk tally of "days without the bell" on the barn wall, ending at 41.

### 3. Bend Crossing Mall, Food Court (T2)
The FEMA shelter, day eleven.
- Rows of cots between food-court tables, and a FEMA intake desk with a stamped clipboard.
- The shutter of a burger counter welded shut from the outside. There are scratch marks on the inside.
- A hand-painted banner reading "BEND CROSSING WELCOMES EVACUEES", with "DON'T" added later in spray paint.

### 4. Ridgeline Correctional, Cell Block C (T3)
The inmates who were locked in turned out to be the survivors.
- Cell doors open, mattresses dragged out to form a ring in the dayroom, and a dominoes game left mid-play.
- A guard's uniform folded neatly on a bunk, with its keys on top.
- The commissary ledger, continued in pencil for two years after the outbreak.

### 5. Camp Teller, Evacuation Gate (T4)
Where the buses stopped.
- Three yellow school buses nose-to-tail at a chain-link gate, with luggage stacked beside them that was never loaded.
- A Guard tent holding a folding table, a stamp and a stack of blank boarding passes.
- A whiteboard reading "NEXT BUS 0600", with "?" written under it in five different handwritings.
- A Guard helmet on a fence post with a bunch of dried flowers in it.

### 6. Meridian Annex, Reactor Control Room (T5)
The last shift chose to stay.
- Control panel lights dead, a coffee mug on the console, and a respirator filter used as a pen holder.
- A shift log on the desk. The final entries are timestamped every ten minutes and get shorter.
- Two chairs pulled together facing the observation window, with a radio between them tuned to KHRV.
- Radiation hazard tape across the inner door, applied from this side.
